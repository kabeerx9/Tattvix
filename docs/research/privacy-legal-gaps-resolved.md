## 1. DPDP Act commencement (G.S.R. 843(E))

Finding. Read the Gazette. Three tranches, all measured from "the date of publication of this notification in the Official Gazette"; the Gazette header is dated 13 November 2025 (the PDF's digital signature is 14 Nov 2025 10:37; treat 13 Nov as the publication date, with 14 Nov as the conservative alternative).
- In force from 13 Nov 2025: s.1(2), s.2 (definitions), ss.18-26 (Board), ss.35, 38-43, s.44(1) and (3). [GSR843] cl.(a).
- One year, i.e. 13 Nov 2026: s.6(9) (Consent Manager registration) and s.27(1)(d) (Board inquiry into Consent Manager registration breaches). [GSR843] cl.(b). Not yet in force today.
- Eighteen months, i.e. 13 May 2027: ss.3-5, s.6(1)-(8) and (10), ss.7-10, ss.11-17, s.27 (except (1)(d)), ss.28-34, 36, 37, s.44(2). [GSR843] cl.(c). This is every substantive duty: notice, consent, s.8 fiduciary duties (security, breach, erasure, grievance), children, SDF, rights, cross-border, exemptions, penalties, and the omission of IT Act s.43A.
- Rules mirror this: r.1(2)-(4) [Rules] p.1 ("Rule 4 shall come into force one year after"; "Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 ... eighteen months after").
- Later amendment: none found. Searched for 2026 Gazette notifications shortening 18 to 12 months; only law-firm commentary on a MeitY proposal (ssrana.in, indialaw.in, Feb 2026) surfaced. No G.S.R. amending 843(E) located on egazette.gov.in via search.

Sources. [GSR843]; [Rules] r.1. Confidence: Confirmed from primary (dates); UNCONFIRMED that no amendment exists (absence of evidence after web search; re-check egazette.gov.in before launch).

## 2. DPDP Rule 8(3): scope and interaction with Act s.8(7)

Finding. Rule 8(3) applies to every Data Fiduciary, not only Third Schedule classes. Text [Rules] r.8(3): "Without prejudice to sub-rules (1) and (2), a Data Fiduciary shall retain, in respect of any processing of personal data undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor, such personal data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing for a minimum period of one year from the date of such processing, for the purposes as specified in the Seventh Schedule, after which the Data Fiduciary shall cause such personal data and logs to be erased, unless further retention is required for compliance with any other law for the time being in force or notified by the Government." Illustration Case 1: e-book platform "must retain the order details, personal data, and logs of the processing (such as order confirmation, payment, and delivery events) for at least one year from the date of the transaction, even if X deletes her account." Case 2: cloud processor C must also retain "the data and associated logs for at least one year before erasure". Seventh Schedule purposes: State use for sovereignty/security (item 1); State use for functions under law or disclosure obligations (item 2); SDF assessment (item 3). Rule 6(1)(e) separately requires logs and personal data kept one year for security investigation.
Plain reading on images: r.8(3) says "such personal data ... of the processing", not "all data in the form collected". Act s.8(7) is subject to "unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law" and r.8(3) is such a law. The text does not distinguish an ID image from its extracted fields. The defensible reading is that the data actually processed must be retrievable for one year; nothing in the text says a copy in a different form (image vs structured record) is required, but nothing says it is not. No ruling or guidance exists. A 7-day image purge with a one-year structured record plus logs is a reasonable position only if counsel agrees the structured record is "such personal data"; otherwise the images would need to be kept one year.

Sources. [Rules] r.8(3), r.6(1)(e), Seventh Schedule; [Act] s.8(7)-(8) (illustration II: bank keeps KYC ten years because law requires). Confidence: Confirmed from primary (text, scope); the image question is interpretation, UNCONFIRMED.

## 3. Immigration and Foreigners Rules 2025, rule 17 and Form III

Finding. Read the Gazette copy. Rule 17(1): "Every keeper of accommodation shall require every foreigner, including an Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder, seeking accommodation in his premises to furnish the necessary particulars for recording, and sign, on his arrival at the accommodation, and at the time of his departure, the date and time of his departure and the address to which he is proceeding, which shall be maintained by the keeper of accommodation electronically for a period of at least one year." 17(2): available "at all times" for inspection on demand by Registration Officer, District Magistrate or police officer not below Head Constable. 17(3): recorded in English or an Indian language. 17(5): keeper transmits "a copy of duly filled in Form III ... electronically to the Registration Officer through the designated online portal or the mobile application" "as soon as may be but not beyond twenty-four hours, after the arrival". 17(6): departure details within 24 hours of departure. 17(7)(a): "accommodation" includes boarding house, club, hostel, paying guest house, sarai, rented accommodation, home stay, tents, hospital, religious/charitable premises etc. 17(7)(b): "sign" includes a thumb impression or other mark for a foreigner unable to write. Nothing defines "sign" to include or exclude electronic signature; the particulars must be kept "electronically", which implies the signed record may be electronic, but this is inference.
Form III fields (complete list, [IFR] Form III, "[Earlier FORM 'C']", see rules 16, 17(5), 18(5)): Photograph; 1. Name and address of the premises; 2. Phone/Mobile of place of stay; Foreigner details: (i) Name in full as in passport, (ii) Nationality, (iii) Passport Number, (iv) Visa details: Visa Number/OCI No., Type, (v) Contact Ph. in India, (vi) Email Id, (vii) Other details/remarks; Arrival details: (i) Arrived from, (ii) Date of arrival, (iii) Time of arrival, (iv) Purpose of visit (Tourism/Business/Employment/Medical/Student/Conference/Others, details), (v) Previous place of stay; Departure details: (i) Date, (ii) Time, (iii) Next destination; plus hospital birth/death intimation. There is no field for a passport or visa copy; only particulars. (The pre-2025 Form C that Goa still hosts had DOB, permanent address, passport issue/expiry and visa dates; those are gone from Form III.)
Who files: the keeper (rule 17(5) "The keeper of the accommodation shall ... transmit"). Rule 2 of the Act defines keeper as the person in charge of management including authorised persons ([IFA] s.2(j), first pass). Nothing bars an agent uploading on the keeper's behalf, but the duty and liability stay with the keeper.

Sources. [IFR] r.17, Form III (pp.28, 33-34). Confidence: Confirmed from primary (rule text, fields, 1-year electronic retention, 24 h, no copy requirement). Electronic signature sufficiency: UNCONFIRMED (not addressed in text).

## 4. Indian guests: central statute and state texts

Finding. (a) Sarais Act 1867: India Code PDF returned 403/504 on every attempt (curl with three retries, WebFetch), so the Gazette text was not read. The Punjab and Haryana High Court judgment quotes s.7 in full: the keeper is bound (1) to notify the nearest police station of infectious disease or death, (2) to give a Magistrate free access and inspection, (3)-(5) cleanliness and repair, (6) watchmen, (7) exhibit a list of charges; s.8 lets the District Magistrate "order reports on certain matters from the keepers"; ss.3 and 5 cover registration before admitting lodgers. No duty to record lodgers' names appears in s.7 as quoted. So the Sarais Act does not itself mandate a guest ID register; a Magistrate could order reports under s.8.
(b) State texts: Goa Registration of Tourist Trade Act 1982 (read, goatourism.gov.in consolidated PDF) regulates registration of hotel-keepers; it contains no guest register or retention clause (grep of the Act for register/guest/visitor/retain found only the registration register). Delhi: the Delhi Police s.28 regulations that licensed lodging houses (and, per Lok Sabha answers, imposed photo-ID copy and CCTV conditions) were repealed by Gazette notification dated 28.06.2025 after the Lt Governor withdrew sanction on 19.06.2025 [Delhi repeal]. Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand, Himachal: no primary text with a guest-register retention period was found; searches returned only news reports of district BNSS s.163 orders (Indore 4.11.2025; Jhabua Aug-Oct 2026; Jalandhar; Rajouri Dec 2025) and Karnataka homestay guidelines (news only). Pattern: Indian-guest ID recording is imposed by time-limited district magistrate orders under BNSS s.163 (formerly CrPC s.144) and licence conditions, not by a standing statute with a retention period.

Sources. [P&H HC] pp.676-677; Goa Act PDF https://goatourism.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Tourism-Trade-Act.pdf; [Delhi repeal]. Confidence: Partially confirmed (Sarais s.7 via court record, not Gazette; Goa and Delhi primary). No state retention period found: UNCONFIRMED after the searches listed.

## 5. Aadhaar

(a) Hotels and copies. The 27 May 2022 UIDAI/PIB release ("Unlicensed private entities like hotels or film halls are not permitted to collect or keep copies of Aadhaar card") was withdrawn by a 29 May 2022 release citing "possibility of misinterpretation" (reported by multiple outlets; the withdrawal release itself was not fetched, pib.gov.in blocks the tool). Statutory basis checked in [Aadhaar Act]: s.29(1) core biometrics; s.29(2) other identity information "may be shared only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and ... regulations"; s.29(3) limits requesting entities; s.29(4) no Aadhaar number "shall be published, displayed or posted publicly"; s.37 penalises intentional disclosure of identity information "collected in the course of enrolment or authentication" to an unauthorised person (3 years / Rs 10,000, company Rs 1 lakh); s.38 is unauthorised access to the CIDR. None of these makes a hotel holding a guest-supplied copy an offence on its face; s.37 is tied to enrolment/authentication data and the Sharing Regulations (reg 5) expressly contemplate "any ... entity which collects Aadhaar number or any document containing the Aadhaar number".
(b) Sharing Regs reg 6 (verbatim): "(1) The Aadhaar number of an individual shall not be published, displayed or posted publicly by any person or entity or agency. (2) Any individual, entity or agency, which is in possession of Aadhaar number(s) of Aadhaar number holders, shall ensure security and confidentiality of the Aadhaar numbers and of any record or database containing the Aadhaar numbers. (3) ... no entity ... shall make public any database or record containing the Aadhaar numbers of individuals, unless the Aadhaar numbers have been redacted or blacked out ... (4) No entity ... shall require an individual to transmit his Aadhaar number over the Internet unless such transmission is secure and the Aadhaar number is transmitted in encrypted form ... (5) No entity, including a requesting entity, shall retain Aadhaar numbers or any document or database containing Aadhaar numbers for longer than is necessary for the purpose specified to the Aadhaar number holder at the time of obtaining consent." Reg 7: contravention of regs 3-6 "shall constitute a violation of sub-section (2) of Section 29".
(c) AOV Regs. Reg 2(mb): OVSE "means any entity desirous of undertaking offline verification of an Aadhaar number holder". Reg 2(mc): "Offline Aadhaar Data" characteristics "including the requirement of masking Aadhaar numbers before storing". Reg 13A(1) (ins. 9.12.2025): "An entity desirous of undertaking Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC verification or Aadhaar Verifiable Credential verification through Aadhaar Application shall apply to the Authority for registration". Reg 14A(1)(b): OVSE "shall not collect, use or store Aadhaar number or biometric information of any individual for any purpose or share offline Aadhaar data with any other entity except in accordance with the Act and Regulations"; 14A(1)(d): report compromise to UIDAI within 72 hours. Reg 16A(2): "No entity shall perform Offline Verification on behalf of another entity or person." 16A(3)-(4): may store offline Aadhaar data with consent; delete verifiably on revocation. Reg 16C(1): no OVSE shall accept Aadhaar "in physical or electronic form (without authentication), as a proof of identity ... without first verifying the digital signature of the Authority" in the secure QR. Reg 20A(1): optional logs "but shall not, in any event, store the Aadhaar number or Virtual ID". Penalty: reg 24(1A) (ins. 9.12.2025) lets UIDAI "impose penalty on the Offline Verification seeking entity" for breach, plus criminal action; no specific penalty for an entity that never registers is stated in the Regulations text; the only hook is reg 16C read with reg 13A (verification via the Aadhaar app requires registration). UIDAI's OVSE page lists "Hospitality and Tourism" as use case 1, says a registered OVSE "may collect and store verifiable credentials shared with the explicit consent", and application is via https://ovse.uidai.gov.in/. MeitY's 15.05.2026 letter (via Delhi circular): "Any entity desirous of undertaking Aadhaar app based Offline Verification ... should register itself as an OVSE", naming "hotel and guest house registration" as a use case.
(d) Masking. No UIDAI circular on masked Aadhaar for hotels/private entities was found (2018 circular list on uidai.gov.in covers banks/AePS/NRIs). The Regulations use masking as a storage safeguard: AOV reg 14(1)(mb) requires requesting entities to redact "the first 8 digits of the Aadhaar number before storing the physical copies"; reg 13(8) lets UIDAI decide which entities "may be allowed to store Aadhaar number or masked Aadhaar number" (so masked numbers are still regulated); Rules r.6(1)(a) names masking as a DPDP safeguard. No text says a masked number falls outside "Aadhaar number".

Sources. [Aadhaar Act] ss.29, 37, 38; [Sharing Regs] regs 5-7; [AOV Regs] regs 2(mb)-(mc), 13(8), 13A, 14(1)(mb), 14A, 16A, 16C, 20A, 24(1A); [UIDAI OVSE]; Delhi circular. Confidence: Confirmed from primary (texts); the 2022 withdrawal is secondary (press reports); "hotels may not keep copies" as a rule of law: UNCONFIRMED, and the advisory was withdrawn.

## 6. Children and companions: Rule 10 and Fourth Schedule

Finding. Act s.9(1): verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data; s.9(3): no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads; s.9(4): exemptions "as may be prescribed". Rule 10(1): the fiduciary must obtain verifiable consent of the parent "and shall observe due diligence, for checking that the individual identifying herself as the parent is an adult who is identifiable if required ... by reference to (a) reliable details of identity and age ... available with the Data Fiduciary; or (b) details of identity and age, voluntarily provided (i) by the individual; or (ii) through a virtual token mapped to such details, which is issued by an authorised entity" (DigiLocker included). Rule 12 and Fourth Schedule Part A exempt only: clinical/mental health establishments and healthcare professionals; allied healthcare professionals; educational institutions; creche/day-care; school transport. Part B exempts purposes: legal functions in the child's interest; subsidies/benefits/licences under s.7(b); email-only account creation; real-time location for safety; blocking harmful content; confirming a principal is not a child. No class or purpose covers a hotel, travel or accommodation platform. A minor companion's ID processed by Tattwix therefore needs verifiable parental consent under r.10; a primary guest who is the parent and whose own ID Tattwix already holds fits r.10(1)(a) (Illustration Case 1).

Sources. [Act] s.9; [Rules] r.10, r.12, Fourth Schedule. Confidence: Confirmed from primary.

## 7. Cross-border: Act s.16, Rule 15

Finding. s.16(1): "The Central Government may, by notification, restrict the transfer of personal data by a Data Fiduciary for processing to such country or territory outside India as may be so notified." s.16(2) preserves stricter sectoral laws. Rule 15: "Any personal data processed by a Data Fiduciary under the Act may be transferred outside the territory of India subject to the restriction that the Data Fiduciary shall meet such requirements as the Central Government may, by general or special order, specify in respect of making such personal data available to any foreign State, or to any person or entity under the control of or any agency of such a State." No restricted-country notification and no r.15 order was found (search of 2026 Gazette coverage; only commentary). Rule 15 is general, not SDF-specific; the SDF-only localisation is r.13(4) (data "specified by the Central Government" on a committee's recommendation must not leave India), which does not bind a non-SDF. Neither s.16 nor r.15 is in force until 13 May 2027.

Sources. [Act] s.16; [Rules] r.15, r.13(4)-(5). Confidence: Confirmed from primary (text); absence of notifications UNCONFIRMED beyond search.

## 8. Significant Data Fiduciary: s.10

Finding. s.10(1): the Centre "may notify any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as Significant Data Fiduciary, on the basis of an assessment of such relevant factors as it may determine, including (a) the volume and sensitivity of personal data processed; (b) risk to the rights of Data Principal; (c) potential impact on the sovereignty and integrity of India; (d) risk to electoral democracy; (e) security of the State; and (f) public order." Duties: India-based DPO, independent data auditor, periodic DPIA and audit (s.10(2)); r.13 adds annual DPIA/audit with report to the Board, algorithmic due diligence, localisation of specified data. Seventh Schedule item 3 authorises MeitY officers to assess candidates. No thresholds in the Act or Rules; no notification naming SDFs or classes found. s.10 is not in force until 13 May 2027.

Sources. [Act] s.10; [Rules] r.13, Seventh Schedule. Confidence: Confirmed from primary; no notification found (UNCONFIRMED absence).

## 9. SPDI Rules 2011

Finding. Read the Gazette text (G.S.R. 313(E), 11 Apr 2011; copy at the Cyber Policy Portal; MeitY's URLs .../GSR313E_10511(1).pdf return 404, WIPO Lex serves an HTML shell). Rule 3 closed list: "(i) password; (ii) financial information such as Bank account or credit card or debit card or other payment instrument details; (iii) physical, physiological and mental health condition; (iv) sexual orientation; (v) medical records and history; (vi) Biometric information; (vii) any detail relating to the above clauses as provided to body corporate for providing service; and (viii) any of the information received under above clauses by body corporate for processing, stored or processed under lawful contract or otherwise", with a public-domain proviso. Passport, Aadhaar and other ID numbers are NOT in the list; a face photo on an ID is not "biometric information" unless processed as such (not defined in the Rules). Rule 4: a body corporate that "collects, receives, possess, stores, deals or handle information" must publish on its website a privacy policy giving (i) practices and policies, (ii) types of personal or sensitive data collected, (iii) purpose, (iv) disclosure per rule 6, (v) security practices per rule 8. Rule 4 applies to "personal information including sensitive personal data", so it applies to Tattwix regardless of rule 3. Rule 5(1): written consent (letter, fax or email) before collecting sensitive data; 5(2) lawful and necessary purpose; 5(3) knowledge of collection; 5(9): "designate a Grievance Officer and publish his name and contact details on its website ... redress the grievances ... within one month". Rule 8: compliance if there is a "comprehensive documented information security programme"; 8(2) "IS/ISO/IEC 27001 ... is one such standard"; 8(4) deemed compliance if certified/audited annually. In force: yes. Act s.44(2)(a) ("section 43A shall be omitted") commences only on 13 May 2027 [GSR843] cl.(c), so s.43A and these Rules remain live until then.

Sources. [SPDI] rr.3, 4, 5, 8; [GSR843] cl.(c); [Act] s.44(2). Confidence: Confirmed from primary (via a mirror of the Gazette text, since MeitY's copy is offline).

## 11. Breach notification: DPDP Rule 7 and CERT-In 2022

Finding. Rule 7(1): on becoming aware, intimate "each affected Data Principal, in a concise, clear and plain manner and without delay, through her user account or any mode of communication registered by her" with (a) description incl. nature, extent, timing; (b) likely consequences; (c) mitigation measures; (d) safety steps for her; (e) business contact of a responder. Rule 7(2): to the Board "(a) without delay, a description of the breach, including its nature, extent, timing and location of occurrence and the likely impact; (b) within seventy-two hours of becoming aware ... or within such longer period as the Board may allow on a request made in writing" (i) updated detail, (ii) facts and reasons, (iii) mitigation, (iv) findings on the person who caused it, (v) remedial measures, (vi) report on principal intimations. Not in force until 13 May 2027.
CERT-In Directions (read): issued under s.70B(6); "(ii) Any service provider, intermediary, data centre, body corporate and Government organisation shall mandatorily report cyber incidents as mentioned in Annexure I to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing such incidents or being brought to notice about such incidents" (email incident@cert-in.org.in, phone 1800-11-4949); "(iv) All service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government organisations shall mandatorily enable logs of all their ICT systems and maintain them securely for a rolling period of 180 days and the same shall be maintained within the Indian jurisdiction"; (i) NTP sync to NIC/NPL; (iii) designate a Point of Contact (Annexure II); "This direction will become effective after 60 days from the date on which it is issued" (i.e. 27 June 2022); non-compliance "may invite punitive action under sub-section (7) of the section 70B". The directions apply to any "body corporate", which covers Tattwix. In force: the direction text and PDF remain on cert-in.org.in; the Directions70B page is currently "Page Requested Currently Not available", and no withdrawal notice was found. Note the 180-day "within the Indian jurisdiction" log rule conflicts with Sentry (US/EU only) and Vercel default logging unless logs are also retained in India.

Sources. [Rules] r.7; [CERT-In] pp.1-4. Confidence: Confirmed from primary (texts); CERT-In continued force: Partially confirmed (no revocation found; site page down).

## 12. Consent Manager relevance

Finding. Rule 4 governs registration and obligations of Consent Managers (net worth Rs 2 crore etc., First Schedule). Act s.6(7): "The Data Principal may give, manage, review or withdraw her consent to the Data Fiduciary through a Consent Manager." It is an option for principals; no provision obliges a fiduciary to integrate one. Rule 4 commences 13 Nov 2026; nothing changes for Tattwix on that date. Confidence: Confirmed from primary ([Act] s.6(7)-(9); [Rules] r.4).

## Changes to the first-pass bottom line

1. Commencement dates are now primary-confirmed: 13 Nov 2026 (s.6(9), s.27(1)(d), r.4) and 13 May 2027 (everything else). The "shortening to 12 months" remains a proposal with no Gazette instrument found.
2. Form III never requires a passport/visa copy; it is particulars only (no DOB or address fields either). The hotel's statutory record can be structured data; Tattwix's 7-day image purge does not collide with rule 17. Rule 17 does require a guest "sign" on arrival and departure particulars, kept electronically one year.
3. Rule 8(3) binds every fiduciary, not just Third Schedule classes; the first pass's "unclear" stands, but the plain text keys retention to the "personal data ... of the processing", so keeping a structured record plus logs for one year while purging images is defensible rather than clearly non-compliant. Still a counsel question.
4. The 2022 UIDAI "hotels cannot keep Aadhaar copies" advisory was withdrawn two days later; the first pass should stop citing it as UIDAI's standing position. The live regime is: Sharing Regs reg 5-6 (notice, consent, confidentiality, retention no longer than necessary) and, since 9 Dec 2025, AOV reg 13A/16A/16C: an entity verifying Aadhaar via the Aadhaar app/secure QR must register as an OVSE, cannot verify "on behalf of another entity", must verify UIDAI's digital signature before accepting Aadhaar as ID, and must not store the Aadhaar number in logs. Implication: Tattwix verifying Aadhaar for hotels would need its own OVSE registration (and hotels theirs); accepting a plain photo of an Aadhaar card without signature verification is outside the regulated path and offers no safe harbour.
5. Masking: no circular exempts masked numbers; it is a storage safeguard (first 8 digits redacted) that regulators expect, not a scope exit.
6. Children: no Fourth Schedule exemption reaches a hotel/travel platform. Minor companions need r.10 verifiable parental consent; the primary guest's verified ID can serve as the parent's identity check under r.10(1)(a).
7. Indian guests: Sarais Act s.7 contains no guest-register duty; Delhi's lodging-house regulations were repealed 28 Jun 2025; Goa's tourist-trade Act has no register clause. Recording is driven by district BNSS s.163 orders and licence conditions. Design for per-hotel configurable retention and never promise a national rule.
8. SPDI Rules 2011 are in force until 13 May 2027 and rule 4 (privacy policy) applies to all personal information, not just the rule 3 list; ID numbers are not "sensitive personal data", so rule 5 written consent is not triggered by them, but a biometric selfie would be.
9. CERT-In: 6-hour incident reporting and 180-day logs kept in India apply now to Tattwix as a body corporate; add an incident runbook and an India-resident log store (Supabase/AWS Mumbai), since Sentry offers only US/EU storage.
10. Vendors: Clerk's named sub-processors are all US-located; Sentry has no India region; Vercel functions default to iad1 unless pinned to bom1. The privacy notice must state US processing for auth, error data and hosting unless regions are changed.
11. Cross-border and SDF: nothing notified under s.16, r.15 or s.10; r.15 is general (not SDF-only); only r.13(4) localisation is SDF-specific.
